# Shared voice input — 13 October 2026

## Audit

The owner reports that background noise interrupts the otherwise working homepage call and authorizes the proposed shared audio upgrade. Baseline: main `3996f0bbee43e8cdeb6ac03ae8ed40f3e9d98ad4`, following the verified homepage release in PR #61.

At the audited baseline, homepage, trainer/subscriber calls and guided tap-to-talk used `SpeechGate`: fixed RMS > .018, speech-band energy >= .5, at least five active frequency bins, sustained for 160 ms. A controlled non-speech spectrum passes this gate at 160 ms. This demonstrates a classifier weakness, not the exact sound on the owner's device. Browser echo cancellation/noise suppression are requested but do not establish voice isolation. Signed-in calls also suppressed capture during answers rather than supporting confirmed spoken interruption. The old browser fixtures used musical tones that the new detector should reject; their microphone fixtures were changed to synthetic speech while retaining the real browser/model path.

## Ordered implementation

1. Pin and host the speech model, runtime and denoiser with the application; no third-party audio upload for local classification. Keep existing model/voice adapters, consent and spend controls.
2. Share capture, local neural speech detection and cleanup across homepage, trainer/subscriber calls and server-transcribed guided replies. Benchmark neural noise filtering; preserve speech, bounded buffers and explicit failure recovery.
3. Use separate speech and interruption confidence/duration. Retain speech onset; pause/resume an answer on a provisional interruption and commit cancellation only after confirmed speech. Mute the microphone track and reset capture; stop microphone, inference and playback on end/navigation cleanup. Do not collect assistant audio for trainer voice samples.
4. Replace tone microphone fixtures with synthetic speech. Verify silence, fans/broadband noise, impacts, instrumental sounds, quiet speech, noisy speech and deliberate interruptions. Keep background voices as a known distinct limitation of speech detection and ordinary denoising; no paid voice-isolation service is enabled by this slice.
5. Pass affected checks, phone/desktop journeys and normal release gates. Merge and verify the exact serving release under standing authority.

## Acceptance and evidence

All five implementation steps are complete. [PR #63](https://github.com/chordsnstrings/trainer_what/pull/63) is merged and live as `fdd9e080bc4b52d3d8d8a19bfeaf30e485e2145a`. All eight final-head PR gates, all seven exact-main gates and serving verification passed. See the release receipt below.

- Pinned Silero v6 and ONNX Runtime execute locally on 32 ms, 16 kHz frames. RNNoise runs at 48 kHz before detection where supported. Model/runtime/denoiser assets and notices are hosted with the application and copied from pinned packages during build/dev. No audio is uploaded for local classification.
- Ordinary speech starts after 64 ms of qualifying frames, with 350 ms of leading audio retained. Calls accept at least 224 ms of voiced audio. Interruption requires stronger confidence: provisional pause at 96 ms, cancellation at 384 ms; a false start resumes the same answer position. These are configured detector thresholds, not measured end-to-end latency.
- Inference is serial and bounded to eight queued frames; audio older than 300 ms is discarded and the recurrent state resets. Repeated overload within five seconds, capture-worklet failure or required-model failure ends the call with recovery copy. Denoiser loading failure retains browser suppression plus required neural detection. Abort/end releases tracks, nodes, pending inference and playback.
- Spoken interruption during playback requires browser-confirmed acoustic echo cancellation. The manual **Let me speak** control remains available. Background voices and vocals can still count as speech; this change does not isolate a selected person.
- Own-voice call samples bypass extra RNNoise filtering and exclude interrupted additions. The separate raw trainer voice recorder is unchanged. Guided server replies now use the same detector and bounded 16 kHz mono WAV; browser-managed on-device recognition remains separate.

Local actual-browser comparison uses seven deterministic synthetic microphone fixtures, ten seconds per case.
